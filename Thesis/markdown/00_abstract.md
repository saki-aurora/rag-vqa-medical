# Abstract

Medical Visual Question Answering (MedVQA) promises a more useful interface for clinical AI than standalone classifiers because it allows clinicians to query images directly in natural language. In gastrointestinal (GI) endoscopy, this capability is especially relevant for high-volume, high-variability workflows such as ulcerative colitis severity grading, polyp assessment, and procedure documentation. However, practical MedVQA remains limited by dataset bias, long-tail label imbalance, weak lexical grounding in free-text generation, and inconsistent clinical faithfulness across question types.

This dissertation investigated these limitations through a staged, reproducible pipeline across HyperKvasir, Kvasir-VQA, Kvasir-VQA-x1, ImageCLEF MEDVQA-GI 2023, and LIMUC. Across these datasets, the evidence consistently showed that constrained and supervised approaches remain more reliable than naive zero-shot open generation for current GI tasks. The work then moved from benchmark diagnosis to method development by building a controlled generative ulcerative colitis severity module and evaluating it against a strong supervised anchor under fixed internal protocols.

On LIMUC internal evaluation, the LoRA-adapted generative `mode1` lane exceeded the supervised baseline on all headline metrics (accuracy `0.7819`, macro-F1 `0.7279`, balanced accuracy `0.7363`, QWK `0.8637`) while preserving parse compliance. External proxy stress testing showed substantial degradation, which bounded claims to internal validity rather than deployment readiness. Building on this controlled severity layer, the dissertation implemented a PICO-grounded GenAI wrapper for physician-style queries with typed contracts, citation linkage, refusal/escalation behavior, and reproducibility audits. In the frozen Chapter 5 pass, the wrapper achieved full citation coverage, strict claim support `0.8696`, and explicit uncertainty signaling under policy constraints.

The dissertation contribution is therefore a bounded translational pathway from GI MedVQA benchmarking to clinician-facing, evidence-aware multimodal support: reliability characterization, constrained generative severity adaptation, and auditable PICO-grounded query answering. The thesis explicitly avoids autonomous clinical deployment claims and instead provides a reproducible baseline and future research agenda for safer medical AI integration.

**Keywords:** medical visual question answering, gastrointestinal endoscopy, colonoscopy, ulcerative colitis, vision-language models, retrieval-augmented generation, clinical decision support
