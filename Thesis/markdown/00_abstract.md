# Abstract

Medical Visual Question Answering (MedVQA) promises a more useful interface for clinical AI than standalone classifiers because it allows clinicians to query images directly in natural language. In gastrointestinal (GI) endoscopy, this capability is especially relevant for high-volume, high-variability workflows such as ulcerative colitis severity grading, polyp assessment, and procedure documentation. However, practical MedVQA remains limited by dataset bias, long-tail label imbalance, weak lexical grounding in free-text generation, and inconsistent clinical faithfulness across question types.

This dissertation investigates these limitations and develops a clinically oriented VQA framework for colonoscopy. The work is grounded in multi-dataset experimentation across HyperKvasir, Kvasir-VQA, Kvasir-VQA-x1, ImageCLEF MEDVQA-GI 2023, and LIMUC, covering both closed-set and generative settings. Repository benchmarks show a clear pattern: supervised or constrained models remain substantially stronger than raw zero-shot VLMs on current GI tasks. In our saved runs, ViLT reaches 0.9089 accuracy on ImageCLEF MEDVQA-GI validation, while Qwen2.5-VL zero-shot raw outputs collapse to 0.0007 accuracy before lexical projection. On HyperKvasir, fine-tuned ResNet50 reaches 0.8789 test accuracy, while BLIP2 zero-shot projected labels remain low (0.0638). On LIMUC severity grading, fine-tuned ResNet50 attains 0.7539 accuracy, macro-F1 0.6829, and QWK 0.8351, with persistent minority-class difficulty.

These findings motivate the thesis direction: move from score-only pipelines toward clinically grounded, evidence-aware multimodal reasoning that combines (1) robust visual recognition, (2) question-aware answer generation, and (3) retrieval-supported justification for higher-risk decisions. The dissertation contributes a structured research agenda, scenario-driven evaluation design, and an implementation pathway for integrating VQA with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) in GI decision support. The long-term objective is an assistant that does not only classify findings, but can explain, qualify uncertainty, and align outputs with clinician workflow and evidence-based care.

**Keywords:** medical visual question answering, gastrointestinal endoscopy, colonoscopy, ulcerative colitis, vision-language models, retrieval-augmented generation, clinical decision support
